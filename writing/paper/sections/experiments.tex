\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate DFM-Fusion on LoCoMo to test whether deterministic fusion can replace LLM-guided fusion as the default memory-maintenance operator under a fixed retrieval and answer-generation pipeline.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\paragraph{Dataset.}
We use LoCoMo-10~\citep{Maharana2024EvaluatingVL}, a subset of the LoCoMo benchmark containing 10 conversations with 1,986 QA pairs. Questions span five categories: multi-hop (282), single-hop (817), temporal (321), open-domain (96), and adversarial (446). Multi-hop F1 serves as our primary metric, as it requires integrating information across multiple memory entries and is most sensitive to fusion quality.

\paragraph{Cross-model protocol.}
The main empirical evaluation is a six-model family study. We run the same memory pipeline across six open-weight answer-generation models served locally via Ollama: \texttt{qwen25\_7b}, \texttt{qwen25\_14b}, \texttt{qwen3\_8b}, \texttt{llama31\_8b}, \texttt{llama32\_3b}, and \texttt{mistral\_7b}. The answer-generation LLM is the sole experimental variable; the memory pipeline (Sentence-BERT embeddings~\citep{Reimers2019SentenceBERTSE}, identical retrieval budgets, and DFM-Fusion or LLM-Fusion operator) is held constant across models. LLM-Fusion maintenance calls (fusion generation and preservation checking) always use \texttt{DeepSeek-V3.2} regardless of which local answer model runs, replicating FadeMem's API-based maintenance design. For each model we compare three conditions: (1)~\textbf{LLM-Fusion}: FadeMem-style fusion with additional \texttt{DeepSeek-V3.2} calls for fusion generation and preservation checking; (2)~\textbf{DFM-Fusion}: our deterministic fusion operator with no maintenance-time LLM calls; and (3)~\textbf{No-Fusion}: no memory merging. All conditions share identical retrieval budgets, scoring protocol, and memory infrastructure.

An additional single-model pilot using \texttt{DeepSeek-V3.2} as the answer model (3 runs per condition, temperature=0) validated the harness and is reported in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix} (Table~\ref{tab:appendix_pilot}) as a sanity check only; it is not part of the main results.

\paragraph{Statistical tests.}
We report mean$\pm$std over 3 runs per condition. Our primary paired comparison is DFM-Fusion vs.\ LLM-Fusion on per-question multi-hop F1 scores. We report mean differences, paired t-test $p$-values, and TOST equivalence outcomes with a pre-specified equivalence margin of $\pm 0.01$ absolute multi-hop F1. This margin corresponds to a practically negligible change relative to the observed No-Fusion to LLM-Fusion gaps on LoCoMo-10. The resulting evidence should be interpreted conservatively: only one model satisfies the configured TOST equivalence criterion, so our main claim is practical competitiveness rather than universal statistical equivalence.

\paragraph{Accuracy results.}
Table~\ref{tab:main_results} shows that DFM-Fusion improves over LLM-Fusion on multi-hop F1 in 5/6 models; the only exception is Qwen2.5-7B, where the gap is negligible ($-0.0007$). The strongest individual result is Qwen3-8B, where DFM-Fusion reaches 0.2157, outperforming LLM-Fusion by $+0.0100$. Averaged across the six families, DFM-Fusion leads by $+0.0036$ on multi-hop F1. The key driver of this advantage is verbatim evidence packing within the retrieval budget, not LLM rewriting --- a mechanism that operates identically across model families, which is why the directional result holds consistently despite the heterogeneity of the six answer models.

We observed that the per-category breakdown makes the scope of this advantage precise. DFM-Fusion leads on multi-hop and single-hop categories, where success rewards redundancy-aware packing of verbatim evidence. LLM-Fusion retains small advantages on temporal, open-domain, and adversarial categories, where success requires paraphrastic synthesis or coarse temporal normalization --- capabilities that verbatim packing cannot replicate. To illustrate where this boundary lies: on a temporal question such as ``When did Caroline join a new activist group?'', DFM-Fusion retrieved topically relevant evidence but could not normalize it into the expected relative temporal expression, returning no date; LLM-Fusion produced a coarse but partially correct relative date. This failure mode is consistent with the average-row temporal gap (DFM 0.0545 vs.\ LLM 0.0571) and marks exactly the category where generative rewriting retains value.

Among the ablation variants, DFM w/ mean embedding is the weakest on multi-hop F1 ($-0.0051$ vs.\ DFM-Fusion) while showing the highest temporal F1 (0.0579). We observed that this reversal is consistent with averaged embeddings over-smoothing local structure: mean embeddings retrieve broader, more topically diffuse evidence that occasionally matches temporal queries but fails on the precise multi-hop integration that exact-text embeddings support. The remaining three ablations stay within 0.0010 of DFM-Fusion on multi-hop F1 and differ negligibly across all categories.

\paragraph{Retrieval and fusion diagnostics.}
Retrieval-side diagnostics support the same direction. Averaged across six models, DFM-Fusion yields slightly stronger evidence recall / hit rate / MRR (0.3667 / 0.4105 / 0.2067) than LLM-Fusion (0.3662 / 0.4098 / 0.2064) and No-Fusion (0.3628 / 0.4070 / 0.2045). Each DFM fusion event packs on average 9.65 selected sentences into 106.64 tokens, with mean coverage of 0.9189 for capitalized entities, 0.8278 for TF-IDF salient terms, 0.8862 for stemmed $n$-grams, and 1.0 for both dates and numeric tokens. Retrieval diagnostics and fusion behavior statistics (Tables~\ref{tab:appendix_retrieval}--\ref{tab:appendix_fusion_behavior}) are identical or near-identical across the six answer models by design: these quantities depend only on the memory pipeline and the shared Sentence-BERT encoder~\citep{Reimers2019SentenceBERTSE}, not on which local model generates answers.

\paragraph{Retrieval-time redundancy baseline.}
To isolate whether the observed gains stem from maintenance-time consolidation or could equally be obtained by query-time diversity control, we evaluated \textbf{No-Fusion+QueryMMR}: query-time MMR reranking applied at every inference call, without any memory fusion.
Averaged across six models in the same experimental run, No-Fusion+QueryMMR achieves 0.1934 multi-hop F1 --- exceeding both DFM-Fusion (0.1885) and LLM-Fusion (0.1828) within the same run --- confirming that deduplication at any stage of the pipeline yields substantial gains.
However, No-Fusion+QueryMMR degrades adversarial F1 by $-0.029$ relative to DFM-Fusion (0.8160 vs.\ 0.8453), because per-query diversity reranking introduces noise on adversarial questions that reward selective non-answers.
DFM-Fusion achieves comparable multi-hop gains through offline maintenance (a one-time cost amortized over all future queries) while preserving adversarial F1.

\paragraph{Operational efficiency.}
DFM-Fusion eliminates all maintenance-time LLM calls, replacing them with CPU-only deterministic operations. This yields a 69.13$\times$ average maintenance speedup, an 11.74\% reduction in total API calls, and a 3.88\% reduction in cost --- at no accuracy cost on the primary metric. Per-model operational breakdowns, retrieval diagnostics, paired tests, TOST outcomes, and targeted error analysis appear in the appendix.

\paragraph{Accuracy results.}
Table~\ref{tab:route_results} shows that DFM-Route leads on multi-hop F1 in 6/6 local models and on temporal F1 in 6/6 models.
The most striking gain is on temporal F1: the six-model average rises from 0.1888 (DFM-Extractive) to 0.2980 (DFM-Route), a $+58\%$ relative improvement.
This directly addresses the temporal limitation identified in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}: by routing temporal queries to conservative anchor-based retrieval and applying date-unit recovery in the verifier, DFM-Route recovers the temporal precision that verbatim packing alone could not achieve.

The overall F1 improvement (0.3637 → 0.4292, $+0.0655$) is driven jointly by temporal and single-hop gains.
With DeepSeek-V3.2 Pro as the answer model, DFM-Route reaches 0.4969 overall F1 --- the highest result across all experiments.

A consistent trade-off appears on adversarial F1: DFM-Route averages 0.7840 versus 0.8261 for DFM-Extractive.
However, we observed that this adversarial gap is identical in character to the adversarial gap of No-Fusion+QueryMMR (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}) relative to DFM-Fusion: expanding the retrieval set introduces more potential for incorrectly supported answers, which adversarial questions penalize.
The adversarial difference is concentrated in models with weak abstention behavior (Llama3.2-3B, Mistral-7B); Qwen2.5-14B retains near-parity (0.9462 vs.\ 0.9641).

\paragraph{Routing statistics.}
Averaged across 1,986 queries on LoCoMo, DFM-Route routes 78\% of queries to the conservative strategy, 12\% to union, and 10\% to expansive.
Temporal queries are the main beneficiary of the temporal guard: among the 292 temporal questions, the anchor-based conservative strategy is selected by default, ensuring that date-bearing sentences from the top memory anchor are always included.
The rule-based verifier keeps 97\% of answers unchanged (1,928/1,986), recovers 2\% from over-abstention, and abstains on 1\%, confirming that it acts as a conservative post-hoc filter rather than an aggressive rewriter.
