\begin{abstract}
To maintain factual coherence across long conversational sessions, memory-augmented agents must continuously consolidate redundant dialogue fragments into compact, retrievable representations --- a process known as memory fusion that accounts for the dominant share of system performance, with its removal collapsing multi-hop F1 by over 53\%. However, fusion universally relies on additional LLM calls at maintenance time, a dependency whose necessity has never been empirically tested. We ask a precise question: is the fusion gain driven by the LLM's linguistic rewriting, or by the simpler effect of deduplicating and packing verbatim evidence into a fixed retrieval budget? To answer it, we propose DFM-Fusion, a fully deterministic operator requiring zero maintenance-time LLM calls. More concretely, DFM-Fusion applies four sequential stages --- sentence segmentation, near-duplicate removal, MMR-based sentence packing, and deterministic coverage verification --- to consolidate memory clusters without any generative rewriting. Key to DFM-Fusion's design is the recognition that verbatim evidence packing, not LLM rewriting, accounts for virtually all of the fusion gain in fact-centric conversational QA. Across six open-weight model families on LoCoMo, DFM-Fusion achieves the best average multi-hop F1 (0.1877) versus LLM-Fusion (0.1841) and No-Fusion (0.1785), improving in 5/6 models while reducing maintenance overhead by 69.13$\times$ and eliminating all fusion-specific API calls. The answer to our question is clear: effective memory consolidation for long-horizon conversational agents can be made entirely free of API dependencies in the maintenance loop.
\end{abstract}
