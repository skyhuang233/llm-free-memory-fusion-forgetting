\begin{abstract}

Generalizable manipulation of articulated objects remains a challenging problem in real-world scenarios given the diverse object structures and complex goals.
%
Inspired by Gibson's theory of distributed cognition, we propose a novel framework that bridges physical and cognitive affordances at the part level to facilitate better comprehension and interaction with various object structures, such as household appliances, under language instructions.
%
At the scene comprehension level, we combine the large Visual-Language Models (VLM) and small perception networks and enforce their coherency to generate context descriptions with both rich information and the correctness of task-related facts.
%
At the interaction level, given a natural language input, we employ a Large Language Model (LLM) to translate the input into programmatic, part-level action units, represented by 3-tuples of part names, joint types, and state changes.
The action units are then translated into executable policies by a part manipulation generator, which takes into account the nature of articulated object interactions.
%
\todo{Experiments will} demonstrate the effectiveness and cross-category cross-task domain generalizations of our framework. We also provide a new benchmark for language-guided articulated-object manipulation tasks in diverse and realistic scenarios.

\end{abstract}
